# The CAGED System

The CAGED system is a way of organising the fretboard around five chord shapes every guitarist already knows: the open C, A, G, E and D major chords. Each of those shapes can be moved up the neck as a movable form, and together the five forms cover the same chord in five overlapping places from the nut to the 12th fret and beyond.

The name is also the order. Going up the neck, the shapes always follow C, A, G, E, D and then start again with C. Learning that cycle turns the fretboard from a set of isolated chord grips into one connected map.

## The five open shapes

| Shape | Open chord | Tab | Root strings |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | C major | `x-3-2-0-1-0` | 5 and 2 |
| A | A major | `x-0-2-2-2-0` | 5 and 3 |
| G | G major | `3-2-0-0-0-3` | 6, 3 and 1 |
| E | E major | `0-2-2-1-0-0` | 6, 4 and 1 |
| D | D major | `x-x-0-2-3-2` | 4 and 2 |

Each shape contains only the three notes of a major triad, root, major third and fifth, arranged differently across the strings. The root strings column matters most: when you move a shape, you place it by its root.

## Each shape as a movable form

Open chords use the nut as part of the shape. To move a shape, replace the nut with a finger (usually a barre) and keep the fretted notes in the same relationship above it. Everything shifts by the same number of frets, so the chord quality stays major while the root changes.

- **E shape:** barre at the root fret on string 6. Barred at fret 3 it becomes G major, `3-5-5-4-3-3`.
- **A shape:** barre at the root fret on string 5. Barred at fret 5 it becomes D major, `x-5-7-7-7-5`.
- **D shape:** root on string 4, played on the top four strings.
- **C shape:** root on string 5 under the ring finger, with a barre a few frets lower covering the open-string notes.
- **G shape:** root on string 6 and string 1, with the barre a few frets below; the full form is a stretch, so players often use just part of it.

The E and A shapes are the standard barre chords. The C, G and D shapes are less common as full grips, but their fragments appear constantly in riffs, fills and chord melodies.

## One chord up the neck: C major

Here is C major in all five shapes, in the order they appear going up:

| Shape | Frets | Tab |
|---|---|---|
| C shape | open (0–3) | `x-3-2-0-1-0` |
| A shape | 3rd fret (3–5) | `x-3-5-5-5-3` |
| G shape | 5th–8th fret | `8-7-5-5-5-8` |
| E shape | 8th fret (8–10) | `8-10-10-9-8-8` |
| D shape | 10th fret (10–13) | `x-x-10-12-13-12` |

After the D shape, the cycle returns to the C shape an octave above the open position, at frets 12–15: `x-15-14-12-13-12`.

The same cycle works for any chord. Only the starting shape changes: for A major, the cycle begins with the open A shape and continues G, E, D, C; for G major it begins with the G shape. The order never changes, so once you know where one shape is, you know which shape comes next above and below it.

## Connecting the shapes

Neighbouring shapes share notes, which is why they lock together. The highest-fret root of one shape is the lowest-fret root of the next:

- The open C shape's root at string 5 fret 3 is the root of the A shape barred at fret 3.
- The A shape's root at string 3 fret 5 is shared with the G shape.
- The G shape's root at string 6 fret 8 is where the E shape's barre goes.
- The E shape's root at string 4 fret 10 is the root of the D shape.

Practising transitions between neighbours, rather than isolated shapes, builds the connected picture. A useful exercise is to play one chord in all five shapes ascending and descending, saying the shape name aloud each time.

## Linking shapes to scale positions

Each CAGED shape sits inside one of the five pentatonic positions, so the chord shape and the scale box are two views of the same fretboard region. For C major, using the A minor pentatonic positions (the same five notes as C major pentatonic):

| C major shape | Fret area | Pentatonic position |
|---|---|---|
| G shape | 5–8 | Position 1 (frets 5–8) |
| E shape | 8–10 | Position 2 (frets 7–10) |
| D shape | 10–13 | Position 3 (frets 9–13) |
| C shape | 12–15 | Position 4 (frets 12–15) |
| A shape | 3–5 | Position 5, an octave down (frets 2–5) |

Every note of each chord shape is also a note of the matching scale position. That makes the shapes a guide for soloing: you can see which scale notes are chord tones, land on them at the ends of phrases, and move to the next position knowing exactly which chord shape lives there.
